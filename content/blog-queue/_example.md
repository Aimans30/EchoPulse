Most agents film a listing once, post it once, and move on. That is the single most expensive habit in real estate marketing, because the walkthrough is the only asset that took real effort to produce.

## Why one walkthrough is enough

A 20-minute walkthrough contains everything a month of content needs: the hook, the proof, the neighbourhood story and the call to action. The job is not to film more. The job is to **cut smarter**.

> Volume comes from editing, not from shooting.

## The cut-down map

Here is how we split a single walkthrough across formats. Each row is one deliverable; the whole table comes from the same raw file.

| Format | Length | Count per walkthrough | Where it goes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Vertical reel | 15–30s | 6 | Instagram, TikTok, Shorts |
| Room-by-room clip | 45–60s | 4 | Instagram carousel, email |
| Full tour | 3–5 min | 1 | YouTube, listing page |
| Talking-head intro | 20s | 2 | LinkedIn, Facebook |
| Still frames | n/a | 10 | Stories, listing cards |

### The three hooks that work

1. **The number hook**: "This 3-bed in Pune listed at ₹1.2Cr and had 14 showings in 48 hours."
2. **The objection hook**: "Everyone says this street is too noisy. Watch the window test."
3. **The reveal hook**: open on the worst angle, cut to the best one.

### What to avoid

- Filming in portrait *and* landscape in one session. Pick one and crop later.
- Long pans. Hold every shot for at least 3 seconds.
- Music-first edits. The voice carries the story; music fills gaps.
