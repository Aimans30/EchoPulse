# EchoPulse: Clientele Pages Playbook

How each of your six services solves each segment's problem, plus the angle, hero message, proof points, and starter stack for every landing page.

Your six services, for reference:

1. **Video Editing** (Cinematic, Short Form, Real Estate)
2. **LinkedIn & Social** (Posts, Carousels, Captions)
3. **Blog Production** (Long-Form, SEO, Researched)
4. **Ad Creatives** (Meta, TikTok, Static + Video)
5. **Websites & Funnels** (Websites, Funnels, Lead Pages)
6. **Automations** (Make.com, ManyChat, CRM)

A quick note on the stats below: I've kept the numbers that come from primary sources (NAR, LinkedIn/Edelman, Dreamdata, HubSpot, Meta, CMI) and flagged anything softer as "directional." Use the hard numbers in headlines, keep the soft ones as supporting lines so nobody can call you on them.

## 1. Founders & Operators (OUR CORE)

### Who they are
SaaS, agency, and consulting founders. Smart, busy, already sold on content but drowning in execution. They're currently juggling a video editor, a ghostwriter, a blog person, maybe an ads freelancer, and stitching it together themselves at 11pm. The pain isn't "should I do content," it's "I'm the bottleneck and I'm tired of being five people's project manager."

### Their real problem
The B2B buy happens before they ever get a call. Roughly 70% of the B2B buying journey is complete before a buyer talks to sales, and the average journey now runs months, multiple stakeholders, and dozens of touchpoints. If a founder isn't visible across that window, they're invisible during the exact period the decision gets made. Content is the only thing that scales presence without scaling the founder's calendar.

### Service mapping (priority order)
1. **LinkedIn & Social** — the engine. LinkedIn's algorithm pushes personal profiles far harder than company pages, which is why founder-led content outperforms brand accounts. This is where the pipeline starts.
2. **Video Editing (short form)** — video posts pull meaningfully more engagement than text/static on LinkedIn, and founder face-to-camera clips build the trust that closes long cycles.
3. **Blog Production** — the SEO layer buyers hit when they research you mid-cycle. Ranks, compounds, and feeds the "3+ pieces of content before a sales call" behavior.
4. **Automations** — DM flows and CRM glue so inbound interest doesn't leak. Lead comes in, gets routed, nothing drops.
5. **Ad Creatives** — amplify the winners once organic proves what resonates.
6. **Websites & Funnels** — the destination that converts the attention into booked calls.

### The transformation
From "I am the content team and I'm burnt out" to "one team ships my LinkedIn, video, blog, and ads, and I review, not produce." They get their evenings back and a pipeline that runs whether they post that day or not.

### Landing page
- **Hero:** "Stop reviewing five freelancers. Run the business." (you already have this line, lean into it)
- **Sub:** "We handle your LinkedIn, video, blog, and ads as one team. You stay the founder, not the production manager."
- **Proof points:** 70% of the B2B buy happens before a sales call. Personal profiles beat company pages on LinkedIn by a wide margin. Buyers consume 3+ pieces of content before they talk to you.
- **Starter stack:** LinkedIn & Social + Short-Form Video + Blog. Add Automations once volume justifies it.

## 2. Real Estate Agents (BIG LIFT)

### Who they are
Solo agents, small brokerages, and short-term-rental operators. They live and die on listings and referrals. Most of them know video matters but either can't shoot/edit it themselves or hired a videographer once, hated the turnaround, and gave up.

### Their real problem
This is your single best data-backed segment. The gap between "video works" and "agents actually use video" is enormous, and that gap is your entire pitch.

### Service mapping (priority order)
1. **Video Editing (cinematic + real estate)** — the whole ballgame. Property reels, listing walkthroughs, personal-brand video. This is your flagship service for this segment.
2. **Ad Creatives** — turn the reels into Meta/Instagram ads that actually book viewings, not just rack up views.
3. **LinkedIn & Social** — consistent posting so the agent stays top-of-mind for referrals between listings.
4. **Websites & Funnels** — a fast listing/lead-capture page, since most buyers start on mobile and bounce on slow sites.
5. **Automations** — lead-in to booked-viewing flows so inquiries don't sit in a DM overnight.
6. **Blog Production** — local-area SEO content (neighborhood guides, market updates) for the agents playing the long game.

### The transformation
From "I post the odd phone photo and hope" to "every listing ships with a cinematic reel and an ad behind it, and viewings get booked while I'm showing another house."

### Landing page
- **Hero:** "The 9 out of 10 agents not using video are leaving money on the table. Be the one who does."
- **Sub:** "Cinematic property reels, personal-brand video, and ads that book viewings. You shoot or we direct, we edit, you list."
- **Proof points (strong, NAR-backed):** Listings with video get up to 403% more inquiries. Homes marketed with video sell faster and for more. 73% of homeowners are more likely to list with an agent who uses video, up from 63% in 2021. Yet only about 1 in 10 agents create listing videos.
- **Starter stack:** Listing Video + Ad Creatives. Add Personal-Brand Social once the listing flow is humming.

## 3. Coaches & Course Creators (HIGH ROI)

### Who they are
Educators, coaches, course sellers. They have an offer and an audience but their launches are manual, exhausting, and feast-or-famine. They're either heads-down filming or heads-down selling, never both, and revenue swings with their energy.

### Their real problem
Cold traffic to a sales page barely converts. The money is in the warm middle: lead magnet, nurture, then offer. They don't have the content volume or the funnel plumbing to run that machine, so they default to manual DMs and live launches that don't scale.

### Service mapping (priority order)
1. **Websites & Funnels** — the core. Lead magnet to opt-in to nurture to booking/sales page. This is what turns scattered content into enrolled students.
2. **Video Editing (short form)** — evergreen clips that fill the top of funnel daily without the coach filming daily.
3. **Automations** — email nurture sequences and DM flows. This is where warm audiences convert at multiples of cold traffic, and it runs without the coach in the loop.
4. **Ad Creatives** — drive cold traffic into the lead magnet, not straight at the offer.
5. **LinkedIn & Social** — authority and pre-launch warming.
6. **Blog Production** — evergreen organic for coaches who want SEO discovery, not just paid.

### The transformation
From "every launch is a manual sprint that drains me" to "evergreen content fills the funnel, the nurture sequence does the selling, and cohorts fill without me in the DMs."

### Landing page
- **Hero:** "Fill your cohort without living in the DMs."
- **Sub:** "Pre-launch content, evergreen short-form, ad creative, and the funnel that nurtures and sells while you teach."
- **Proof points:** Warm, nurtured audiences convert several times higher than cold traffic to a sales page. Webinar and lead-magnet funnels generate roughly 2x the qualified leads of a plain landing page. Video nurturing lifts qualified-lead rates. Cost per lead from a webinar funnel is a fraction of paid events.
- **Starter stack:** Funnel + Automations (nurture) + Short-Form Video. Add Ad Creatives to scale once the funnel converts.

## 4. DTC & E-Commerce

### Who they are
Brands running paid social. They're not asking whether ads work, they're bleeding because creative dies faster than they can replace it. By the time they brief a new batch, the current one's CPA has already spiked.

### Their real problem
Creative fatigue is now the bottleneck, not budget. Ad performance decays in days, not weeks, and traditional production (brief, shoot, edit, 5-10 business days, hundreds of dollars per asset) can't keep up. Your subscription model is the literal fix for this. That's the whole pitch.

### Service mapping (priority order)
1. **Ad Creatives (static + video, subscription)** — the core offer. Fresh hooks shipped before fatigue kills CPA. This segment exists for this service.
2. **Video Editing** — UGC-style and short-form video variants, which consistently outperform polished brand content on cost and conversion.
3. **Websites & Funnels** — store, landing pages, and post-click experience that doesn't waste the click.
4. **Automations** — email/SMS flows (abandoned cart, post-purchase) where the margin actually lives.
5. **LinkedIn & Social** — organic presence and founder/brand story for the brands that want it.
6. **Blog Production** — SEO product/education content for brands playing the organic long game.

### The transformation
From "we burn through creative every two weeks and scramble" to "fresh static and video variants land on a subscription, we test, the winners scale, CPA stays sane."

### Landing page
- **Hero:** "Creative dies in days. We refill the tank on subscription."
- **Sub:** "Fresh static and video ads every week, plus the store, the funnels, the email. Fatigue stops killing your CPA."
- **Proof points:** Ad frequency past ~3.0 reliably drags CTR down. TikTok creative lifespan is roughly 7-14 days. UGC-style ads consistently beat polished brand content on conversion and CPM. Brands refreshing creative weekly run meaningfully lower CPMs than monthly-refresh brands (directional, from agency diagnostics). Performance teams run 15-25 active variants per campaign, which is exactly what subscription production unlocks.
- **Starter stack:** Ad Creatives subscription + Video Editing. Add Automations (email/SMS) to lift LTV.

## 5. Business Owners

### Who they are
Local businesses, service operators, small agencies, consultancies. They're sick of cold outreach, chasing, and paying for leads that don't close. They want the phone to ring because someone found them and already trusts them.

### Their real problem
Outbound is getting more expensive and less effective every year, while inbound (content, SEO, a site that converts) costs less per lead and closes far better. They just don't have the time or the system to produce content consistently, so they stay stuck on the outreach treadmill.

### Service mapping (priority order)
1. **Blog Production (SEO)** — the inbound engine. Ranks, compounds, and is the highest-ROI channel for this segment.
2. **Websites & Funnels** — a fast, conversion-built site that turns the traffic content earns into actual leads. Speed matters here.
3. **Automations** — capture and route every inbound lead so nothing slips while they're running the business.
4. **LinkedIn & Social** — local visibility and credibility.
5. **Video Editing** — short-form to build trust and feed social.
6. **Ad Creatives** — paid to accelerate once the organic base is proven.

### The transformation
From "I chase leads with cold outreach and a site that doesn't convert" to "content brings them in already warm, the site converts them, and the system captures every one."

### Landing page
- **Hero:** "Get inbound leads instead of chasing cold ones."
- **Sub:** "Content that ranks, a site that converts, and automations that catch every lead while you run the business."
- **Proof points:** Inbound leads cost roughly 60% less than outbound. Content marketing generates around 3x the leads at a fraction of the cost (CMI/Demand Metric). SEO leads close near 14.6% vs ~1-2% for cold outbound. Website/blog/SEO ranks as the top ROI channel in HubSpot's State of Marketing. Sites loading under 2 seconds convert meaningfully better (ties straight to your sub-2-second Websites claim).
- **Starter stack:** Blog/SEO + Websites & Funnels + Automations.

## 6. Something Else?

### Who they are
The offer that doesn't fit a box. They have an audience, a thing to sell, and no time to manage five vendors. They don't need a template, they need a plan.

### How to handle the page
Keep this one short and human. No stat wall. The whole point is flexibility.

- **Hero:** "Don't see your exact business? You're still in the right place."
- **Sub:** "If you've got an offer, an audience, and no time to chase five vendors, we can probably help. Tell us what you ship and what you want to grow, and we'll hand you a plan, not a pitch."
- **Body:** One line on how you work (one team, all six services, you pick what you need), then straight to the call CTA.
- **CTA:** "Tell us what you're building" / "Book a free call"
- **Starter stack:** Whatever the call surfaces. The page's job is just to get the conversation started.

## Build notes for the pages

- **One hero promise per page.** Each segment has a different pain. Don't reuse the homepage line, write to the specific frustration (burnt-out founder, agent who gave up on video, coach stuck in the DMs, DTC brand bleeding on CPA, owner sick of cold calls).
- **Put the hardest stat in or near the hero.** Real estate's "403% more inquiries" and "only 1 in 10 agents use video" are your strongest, use them boldly. For the others, lead with the behavior stat (70% of B2B buy happens pre-sales, inbound costs 60% less) and keep softer numbers as supporting lines.
- **Show the relevant work.** Each page should pull the matching examples from your portfolio: reels for real estate, carousels/video for founders, funnels for coaches, ad variants for DTC.
- **Same CTA everywhere.** "Book a Free Call." One conversion goal per page.
- **Don't claim numbers as your own results.** These are industry figures showing the opportunity. Frame them as "here's what's true in your market," not "here's what we got you," until you have case studies to swap in.
